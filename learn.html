01 · STOCKS

Stocks from first principles

A stock represents an ownership interest in a company. Learning stocks properly means understanding ownership, the underlying business, financial statements, valuation, market mechanics, and the specific transaction you are entering.

What is a share?

A share is a unit of ownership in a company. Depending on the share class and company structure, shareholders may have rights such as voting and receiving declared dividends. The market price is not the same thing as the intrinsic or fundamental value of the business.

Primary vs secondary market

In a primary offering, securities are issued to investors by the issuer. In the secondary market, investors trade existing securities with other market participants. An exchange is a marketplace; it does not automatically make every security or transaction Shariah-compliant.

How investors make money

Two common sources are capital appreciation and dividends. Neither is guaranteed. A share can lose substantial value, and a company can reduce, suspend or eliminate dividends.

Financial statements

Learn the income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement. Look for revenue quality, margins, operating cash flow, debt, liquidity, dilution and unusual accounting items. Do not rely on a single ratio.

Valuation

Common frameworks include earnings multiples, cash-flow models, book value and comparable-company analysis. A low multiple does not automatically mean a stock is cheap, and a high multiple does not automatically mean it is bad.

Orders and execution

Market orders prioritize execution, while limit orders specify a price boundary. Bid, ask, spread, liquidity and slippage affect the actual execution price. Understand the order before placing it.

Stocks through a Shariah lens

Islamic stock analysis has at least two layers: the company's underlying activity and the company's financial/contractual structure. A company should be investigated rather than judged solely by its ticker, brand or sector label. AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21 provides a formal framework concerning shares and securities; its treatment depends on the nature and composition of the underlying assets and activities.

That means 360HH will not display a universal “halal stock” badge without stating the screening methodology, data date and source. Different scholars and screening methodologies can produce different outcomes, and a current company profile can change.

02 · CRYPTO

Crypto without the hype

Crypto is a broad category, not one single asset class. Bitcoin, smart-contract platforms, stablecoins, governance tokens, utility tokens, NFTs and yield products can have very different structures and risks.

Blockchain basics

A blockchain is a distributed record system in which transactions are recorded according to the network's consensus rules. Learn wallets, addresses, private keys, confirmations, fees and network finality before buying anything.

Coins, tokens and networks

A native coin usually belongs to its own network. A token may be issued on another blockchain. The label alone tells you little about rights, utility, ownership, governance or economic exposure.

Custody

Self-custody means you control the private keys; custodial platforms control them on your behalf. Losing a seed phrase can permanently destroy access. Never share a seed phrase or private key with a person, website or “support agent.”

Tokenomics

Study supply, issuance, unlocks, concentration, utility, governance, treasury holdings, incentives and who receives new tokens. A rising chart does not prove a sustainable economic model.

Crypto products

Spot ownership is different from lending, staking, perpetual contracts, futures, options, margin, leveraged tokens, CFDs and other products. Shariah analysis must examine the actual contract and rights rather than the word “crypto.”

Why Shariah analysis is unsettled

The International Islamic Fiqh Academy's Resolution 237 records unresolved questions about the nature, value, tradability and risks of digital currencies and calls for further research. 360HH therefore treats crypto as a research area, not a blanket halal/haram category.

Crypto research checklist

  1. Identify exactly what you own and what rights it gives you.
  2. Read the project's documentation and verify the team, code, governance and token distribution.
  3. Identify whether the return comes from ownership, a service, lending, interest, leverage, derivatives or speculation.
  4. Check custody, smart-contract, bridge, exchange and counterparty risks.
  5. Check the current scholarly/standards position and record uncertainty instead of hiding it.

03 · ISLAMIC FINANCE

The principles behind halal wealth

Islamic finance is not simply “making money without interest.” It asks how wealth is acquired, what is being exchanged, who owns what, what obligations exist, and whether prohibited elements are present.

Riba

Riba includes prohibited forms of increase in lending and certain exchanges. Qur'an 2:275 explicitly distinguishes permitted trade from forbidden riba. A product should therefore be examined by its contract and economics, not by its marketing name.

Gharar

Gharar refers to prohibited uncertainty/ambiguity in transactions. The practical question is whether important contractual subject matter, delivery, ownership, price or obligations are materially unclear in a way that Islamic law does not permit.

Maysir

Maysir is gambling. Qur'an 5:90 commands believers to avoid gambling. A financial activity should not be treated as acceptable merely because it uses charts, analysis or sophisticated technology if its economic structure is essentially a wager prohibited by Shariah.

Ownership & possession

Ownership, possession and the ability to dispose of an asset matter in many Islamic contracts. This is why “I clicked buy” is not enough to determine the Shariah character of a transaction.

Permissible business

Investing in a company requires attention to what the company actually does. A financially screened company can still require separate consideration if its core business is prohibited.

Scholarly disagreement

Where qualified scholars disagree, 360HH should show the evidence and disagreement rather than manufacture certainty. Personal circumstances and the exact contract can also change a ruling.

Core primary texts

Qur'an 2:275: Allah states that trade is permitted and riba is forbidden. Qur'an 4:29: wealth should not be consumed wrongfully, and trade should occur by mutual consent. Qur'an 5:90: believers are commanded to avoid maysir.

04 · RISK

Risk before return

Risk management is not a promise of safety. It is the discipline of understanding what can go wrong, how much can be lost, and whether the transaction fits your actual capacity.

Risk capacity

Risk capacity is what your finances can withstand. Emergency needs, debt obligations, dependants and time horizon matter. It is different from simply saying you are comfortable with volatility.

Position sizing

Position size should follow a predefined loss budget and the distance to the invalidation/stop level. The 360HH calculator is educational and does not determine what percentage you should risk.

Drawdown

Drawdown measures decline from a previous peak. A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to return to the starting amount, which illustrates why loss control matters.

Concentration

Holding one company, one token, one sector or one platform creates concentration risk. Diversification can reduce some risks but cannot remove market-wide or systemic risk.

Leverage

Borrowing or leveraged exposure magnifies both gains and losses and can introduce interest, liquidation and contractual issues. It requires separate Shariah analysis.

Fees and friction

Trading fees, spreads, slippage, taxes, currency conversion and custody costs can materially change results. A theoretical return before costs is not the same as realized wealth.

05 · RESEARCH

How to investigate an asset

Good research is a repeatable process. The objective is not to find a reason to buy; it is to discover what the asset actually is, what can invalidate the thesis, and what information remains unknown.

1. Identify

Write the exact company, ticker, token, contract, platform and transaction type. “Crypto” or “stocks” is too broad for a serious conclusion.

2. Understand the business

What does it sell? Who pays? What assets does it own? What are its major costs, competitors and dependencies? Look beyond promotional language.

3. Inspect the numbers

For companies, review revenue, profits, cash flow, debt, cash, dilution and relevant disclosures. For tokens, inspect supply, issuance, treasury, unlocks, holders and utility.

4. Inspect the contract

Ask what exactly happens when you buy, sell, lend, stake, borrow, short or use leverage. Product names are not contracts.

5. Verify sources

Prefer primary filings, official documentation, audited reports, recognized standards and direct statements. Separate evidence from influencers' opinions.

6. Record uncertainty

Use “unknown” when evidence is missing. A disciplined research note can say “I do not know yet.” That is stronger than inventing certainty.

Research record

For each review, record the asset, date, source links, business activity, financial structure, transaction type, Shariah questions, security risks and unresolved issues. Re-check when the company, protocol, product or standard changes.

06 · SECURITY

Protect the wealth you build

A profitable strategy is irrelevant if your account, wallet or identity is compromised. Security is part of financial literacy.

Private keys & seed phrases

Never share them. Legitimate support should not require your seed phrase. Store backups offline and protect them from theft, fire and unauthorized access.

Phishing

Attackers imitate exchanges, wallets, banks and people you trust. Do not sign in through links sent unexpectedly. Verify the domain independently.

2FA

Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication where available. Prefer authenticator apps or hardware security over SMS when the service provides stronger options.

Scam patterns

Guaranteed returns, urgency, secret signals, “double your money,” fake withdrawals, impersonation and requests to pay a fee before releasing funds are major warning signs.

Platform risk

An exchange is a counterparty. Understand withdrawal rules, custody arrangements, proof-of-reserves limitations, regulatory status and what happens if the platform fails.

Social engineering

Do not let a persuasive message replace verification. A person can sound professional and still be fraudulent. Verify identities through an independent channel.

Security rule

If someone pressures you to act immediately, stop. Verify independently. Never trade security for speed.
